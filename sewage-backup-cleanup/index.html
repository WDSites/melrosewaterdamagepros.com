Sewage backup cleanup is the removal of wastewater that has come up through a floor drain, toilet, tub or laundry standpipe, along with everything porous it soaked. In Melrose it most often happens in basements, because the lowest drains sit closest to the sewer line when it blocks or overloads. Call our 24/7 emergency line as soon as you see it, and keep everyone out of the room.

Until a crew is there, do not run water, flush toilets or use the washer, because every drain in the house feeds the same blocked line. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.

Sewage is Category 3 water from the first minute

The restoration trade sorts water into three categories. Clean supply water is Category 1, used appliance water is Category 2, and sewage is always Category 3, meaning it carries contamination no matter how clear it looks or how fast you noticed it. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard uses the same three-part scale, and it is the reference to ask any company about.

That classification drives the whole job. Nothing is dried in place and saved on hope, because sewage soaks into material and cannot be rinsed out of it. The question for every surface is whether it can be cleaned and sanitized, or whether it has to leave the house.

Water is coming up a drain or toilet. What is happening?

Wastewater rises through the lowest opening in the plumbing. In a basement that is usually a floor drain, a laundry tub, a shower stall or a toilet. Gray-brown water, a strong odor, gurgling after a flush and slow drains in several fixtures at once all point to a blocked or overloaded line rather than one clogged sink.

Where the blockage sits decides who fixes it. A clog in your own pipe between the house and the street is the owner's responsibility, while a blocked public main belongs to the city. We handle the cleanup in both cases, but you need to know which one you have before anyone digs or snakes a line.

Safety steps in the first hour

Sewage is a contact hazard, so the goal is to limit who and what touches it.

  • Keep everyone out of the affected room, and keep children and pets away from the water and from wet surfaces.
  • Stop using sinks, tubs, toilets and the washer, so no more water goes down a line that is already backing up.
  • If water is near the panel, an outlet or a gas appliance, stay out and call the utility or 911 about the electrical or gas risk before you call us.
  • Do not walk through the water, and do not try to wet-vacuum it with a household vacuum.
  • Take photographs of the waterline and damaged items from the doorway, then call the emergency line.

How a sewage cleanup runs

  1. Assess and containThe crew checks for electrical hazards, finds the water line and seals off the room with plastic so contamination stays put.
  2. Protect the crewTechnicians wear waterproof gear, gloves and respirators, and set up filtered air movement away from clean rooms.
  3. Extract the waterPumps and extraction units pull out standing wastewater, and the floor is rinsed where the surface allows it.
  4. Remove porous materialCarpet, pad, insulation and wallboard that soaked in sewage are cut out, bagged and carried out.
  5. Clean and sanitizeHard surfaces and framing are cleaned first, then treated with an antimicrobial product that fits the surface.
  6. Dry and verifyAir movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and moisture readings confirm it before anything is rebuilt.

What is thrown away and what is cleaned

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health homeowner guide says porous materials contaminated by sewage or flood water, such as wallboard, wooden trim and insulation, should be discarded. Carpet and ceiling tiles that cannot be cleaned and dried effectively go as well. Concrete, block, metal, glass and sealed hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized and usually stay.

Belongings follow the same rule. Hard plastic bins and metal shelving can be washed. Cardboard, books, upholstered furniture and stuffed items that sat in the water are usually discarded, and it helps to photograph each one before it goes so your insurer has a record.

Melrose backups on record

CBS Boston reported that a sewer main blockage on Brazil Street in June 2019 sent sewage into at least four homes. The mayor said the investigation found tree roots and grease in a pipe that was 113 years old, and the city waived permit costs for the homeowners' repairs. The lesson for owners is that old pipe, roots and grease are all ordinary causes, and a permit may be part of the repair.

Melrose's 2017 to 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report, a state document, also lists sanitary sewer overflows as a local vulnerability. That does not tell you where the next one will be. It does mean a backup during heavy rain is a known pattern, and it is worth knowing where your main drain and any floor drains are before a storm.

Insurance and sewage backups

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, and some coverage may exist through an endorsement for water backup or sump pump failure. Whether a backup counts as a covered loss depends on that wording, so read your declarations pages or ask your agent before assuming either way.

Keep records from the first hour. Dated photographs, a list of discarded items and the written scope from the crew all help, and so does a note of who you called about the main and when.

Mistakes to avoid after a backup

The most common mistake is running water to flush the problem away. Another is entering the room barefoot to rescue boxes, which tracks contamination through the rest of the house. Wet-vacuuming sewage with a household vacuum is a third, because the machine and its exhaust spread what it picks up.

People also wait to see whether the water drains on its own, then leave wet carpet in place for days. Mold can follow a loss that stays wet, which is why the state guide asks for drying within 24 to 48 hours where possible. Our flood damage restoration page covers the outside-water cases, and structural drying covers the equipment that follows cleanup.
