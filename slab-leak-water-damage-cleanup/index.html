Emergency Slab Leak Water Damage Cleanup in Melrose, MA

A leak in or under a slab is rare here, but the water damage is real. The 24/7 emergency line is open.

A slab leak is a leaking pipe in or beneath a concrete floor slab, and the water damage it causes shows up in the flooring above. Compared with regions where houses are built on slabs, these leaks are uncommon in Melrose, because most homes here sit over basements. The cases that do happen involve additions, garages, some ranch or split-level houses, and heating tubing or supply lines run through a slab.

If water is surfacing on a floor right now, shut off the main valve and call our 24/7 emergency line. A plumber with leak detection equipment locates and fixes the pipe, and we dry the floor system and clean up what got wet.

How common is a slab leak in Melrose, really?

Not very common, and we would rather say so than sell you a service you do not need. Basement leaks, ceiling leaks and appliance failures are far more typical here. A slab leak needs a slab, which means a slab-on-grade addition, an attached garage, a basement floor with lines under it or a floor with radiant heat tubing embedded in the concrete.

Older houses in Melrose that were built during the railroad commuter era often gained rear additions later, and some of those sit on slabs. If you have one, you are in the group this page covers. If you have a flooded basement instead, our water damage cleanup page is the better fit.

The floor is warm or damp and the meter keeps moving. What does it mean?

A warm spot on a floor with no heat source, damp carpet or flooring over a slab, a hissing sound at night and a water bill that jumps without a reason are the classic signs. A damp patch where a wall meets the floor can also be a slab line leaking into the wall base.

Try a simple check. Turn off every tap and appliance, then look at your water meter. If it still moves, water is going somewhere. Ask your water utility to help check your meter, since Melrose buys its supply from the MWRA and distributes it through its own city system of about 90 miles of pipe.

First-hour safety steps

Water and a concrete floor sound harmless, but wiring and heating equipment are often close by.

  • Shut off the main water valve, then open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain the lines.
  • Stay out of any wet room where water has reached outlets, baseboard wiring or a panel, and call the utility or 911 before you call us.
  • If a radiant heating system feeds the slab, turn that heating zone off, and keep gas appliances away from wet floors.
  • Lift rugs, boxes and furniture off the damp floor and photograph the wet area.
  • Call the emergency line and a plumber, in that order or together, since they handle different parts.

How we handle the water damage

  1. Map the moistureMeters and thermal readings show how far water has spread under and around the flooring.
  2. Stop further spreadWet area rugs, pad and baseboard are pulled so water does not wick into the walls.
  3. Dry the floor systemAir movers, floor drying mats and dehumidifiers pull moisture from flooring and the slab surface.
  4. Open walls if neededWall bases are opened where readings stay high, and wet insulation is removed.
  5. Check for growthIf the loss has been wet for days, the crew looks for mold under flooring and works to the S520 standard if it appears.
  6. Hand off to repairOnce readings are normal, finishes and flooring are scoped separately for replacement.

Insurance fit for a slab leak

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says water damage from causes such as a burst pipe is usually covered when it is sudden and accidental, though the insurer investigates the cause. A slow leak that went on for months is a different question, and gradual damage is often excluded. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and note that a slab leak may fall in between, so read the wording.

Keep your water bills. A pattern of high use can matter to the insurer, and the date of the first sign helps to document when the loss started.

Mistakes to avoid and ways to prevent slab leaks

The first mistake is waiting. A damp floor feels minor, but moisture under flooring spreads sideways and upward. The second is covering the area with a rug or a new floor, which traps water. The third is assuming the leak is in the wall and cutting open the wrong place.

Prevention is limited, since the pipes are buried. Know where your main valve is, watch your water bill, and ask a plumber about the pipe material if you have an addition on a slab. If a basement floor has a heated zone, have it checked before winter.
