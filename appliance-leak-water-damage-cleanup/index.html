Appliance leak water damage cleanup covers the water that escapes from a washing machine, dishwasher, refrigerator ice-maker line or similar fixture, plus everything it soaks on the way. In Melrose houses and apartments, these leaks usually start in a laundry corner or a kitchen, then spread under flooring and into cabinet bases where nobody sees them. If water is moving right now, shut the supply valve and call our 24/7 emergency line.

A small leak that runs for weeks does as much harm as a burst hose that runs for an hour. Our crews find where the water went, remove what cannot dry, and dry the rest. We explain the scope and price before work starts, and same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

The puddle by the machine is only part of the story

Water from an appliance rarely stays where you see it. It follows the flooring seams, slips under the toe-kick of the cabinets and wicks into the bottom edge of drywall. A floor that feels merely damp on top can be wet in the subfloor underneath.

That is why a quick mopping often fails. The surface looks dry by evening, but the hidden layers stay wet, and a musty smell shows up a few days later. The cleanup starts by finding every place the water traveled, not just the first wet spot.

Where appliance leaks usually start

Each source leaves a different pattern of damage, which tells the crew where to look.

Washing machine hoses

Rubber supply hoses split or loosen at the fitting, and drain hoses slip out of the standpipe. Laundry corners often sit over a finished room or an apartment below.

Dishwashers

A worn door seal, a loose drain line or a failed inlet valve leaks into the cabinet base. Water often wicks into the flooring beside the machine.

Refrigerator ice-maker lines

A small tube behind the fridge can drip for weeks. The floor under the appliance stays wet while the rest of the room looks fine.

Under-sink and supply lines

A leaking shutoff valve or a flexible line drips into the cabinet floor, where particleboard swells and softens.

Drain pans and condensate trays

Appliance pans overflow or crack, and the water runs under the unit and along the wall.

First-hour safety steps before you touch anything

Water and electricity do not mix, and appliances sit close to both. Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and the floor around it are dry. If water is pooled near the plug, the outlet or the breaker panel, stay out of the area and call the utility or 911 about the electrical risk before you call us.

Then close the supply valve to the appliance. Washers usually have two small valves on the wall, and dishwashers and fridges use a valve under the sink or behind the unit. If a valve will not turn, shut the main water valve for the house instead.

Move rugs, boxes and small furniture off wet floors, and photograph the area before anything is moved. Leave a heavy appliance where it sits unless you can pull it out safely, because the crew will want to see the floor underneath.

How the cleanup works

  1. Locate the waterMoisture meters and a thermal camera show how far the water traveled under the flooring, behind toe-kicks and into walls.
  2. Extract standing waterA wet vacuum or small extraction unit pulls water off the floor and out of the cabinet base before it sinks deeper.
  3. Remove what will not drySoaked padding, swollen toe-kick boards and wet insulation behind the wall are cut out and bagged. Cuts follow the readings, so they stay small.
  4. Dry the structureAir movers push dry air across the floor and a dehumidifier pulls moisture out of the room. Small injection panels can dry the cavity under a cabinet.
  5. Verify and hand offReadings are logged until the materials match unaffected areas. The rebuild is scoped separately, so you can see each part of the job.

Drying under cabinets and behind the machine

The hardest part of an appliance leak is the space you cannot reach. Cabinet bases sit on the floor, and the toe-kick board hides the gap where water collects. Particleboard cabinet floors swell when wet and rarely return to shape, so they are often replaced.

Where the cabinet is solid and the water is clean, the crew can pull the toe-kick, drill small vent holes and dry the cavity in place. Behind a washer, wallboard that wicked water above the baseboard is checked at several heights before anyone decides how much to open.

Insurance fit: sudden leaks versus slow ones

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance reports that 48.6 percent of homeowners' claims in 2024 were for non-flood water losses and freezing damage, so appliance leaks are a common claim type. Its guidance is that water damage from a burst pipe or failed hose is often covered when it is sudden and accidental, and the insurer investigates the cause.

A leak that dripped for months is a different question, and gradual damage and neglect are often excluded. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph the area, the appliance and the water line before any cleanup begins. Keep the failed hose or part if you can.

Mistakes to avoid after an appliance leak

The common mistake is drying only the top of the floor. A fan and a mop leave the subfloor wet, and mold needs moisture. The Massachusetts Department of Public Health guide for homeowners says wet areas should be dried completely within 24 hours and that mold can start after that.

Others to avoid: pushing the appliance back before the floor underneath is dry, running the machine again to see whether it leaks, and sealing a wet cabinet base with new flooring. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water, and never reach into water near a plug.

Repairs, permits and the other trades

Restoration dries and rebuilds, but it does not fix the appliance. A failed hose or valve is a job for you, an appliance technician or a plumber. In Melrose, the Department of Inspectional Services issues the plumbing permits, and ordinary repairs, carpeting and painting are exempt from permits, so ask the department before replacing a supply line or a drain.

Water heaters fail differently and have their own page, so if the leak is a tank, see water heater leak cleanup. For the general process, our water damage cleanup page walks through removal and sanitizing, and the kitchen water damage page covers the cabinets and floors around a dishwasher.
