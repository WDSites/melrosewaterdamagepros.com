How dispatch across towns works

Call the 24/7 emergency line from any of these towns and the dispatcher asks where you are, what the water touched and where it started. The location tells the crew which roads and highways to take, which is why Route 128 matters for Wakefield and Route 1 for Saugus.

Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and the scope and price are explained before work starts. No arrival time is promised, because a flood across several towns changes what is possible, but the dispatcher keeps you updated.

What to do before a crew arrives, in any town

These steps are the same everywhere we dispatch.

  • Shut off the main water valve if you can reach it safely.
  • Do not touch wet outlets, the panel or appliances standing in water.
  • Avoid walking through standing water that may be sewage or flood water.
  • Lift valuables off wet floors and photograph the damage.
  • Call 911 or the utility first for any electrical or gas risk.

Other places we are called from

Crews also respond to Melrose Highlands, which is part of Melrose and shares its ZIP code. We also get calls from Reading, ZIP 01867, Woburn, ZIP 01801, and Revere, ZIP 02151. Those towns do not have their own page, but the same line answers.

What each town page covers

Every town page lists the neighborhoods we dispatch to, the ZIP code, a local water or flooding fact and the services that fit the town's most common problems. Wakefield centers on Lake Quannapowitt and the Route 128 corridor, while Malden deals with dense multi-unit housing.

Stoneham pages follow seasonal pipe and ice dam problems, Saugus pages cover tidal and river flooding in Essex County, and Medford pages cover the Mystic River side of the area. The same services apply everywhere, and the dispatcher can confirm which page fits your situation when you call.
