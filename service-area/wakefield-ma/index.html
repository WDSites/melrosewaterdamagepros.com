When water is spreading in Wakefield

Water that is still moving is an emergency, whether it comes from a burst supply line in a Greenwood kitchen or a failed sump pump in a Woodville basement. Every minute adds wet drywall, soaked insulation and a longer drying job. The 24/7 emergency line is answered at night, on weekends and on holidays, and same-day response is offered when crews are available.

Stop the source first if you can reach it safely. Then call before you start pulling up carpet or tearing out walls, because what comes out and what stays changes the scope. If sewage is involved or water has reached an outlet or panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us.

If water is still spreading, do this before we arrive

These five moves protect your home and your family while the crew is on the way. None of them needs tools, and none should put you near live electricity.

Find the shutoff

Look for the main water valve, usually where the line enters the cellar, and turn it fully closed. A stopped source keeps the wet area from growing.

Check the panel from a dry spot

Switch off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Otherwise leave it alone and phone the utility.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, boxes, laptops and small furniture onto a dry counter or an upper floor. Photograph anything you move for your records.

Keep everyone clear

Children and pets stay out of any water that came from a drain, a toilet or the ground outside. That water is treated as contaminated.

Where Wakefield water comes from

About 85 percent of Wakefield's water is supplied by the MWRA through two connections. The town supplies the remaining 15 percent from its Broadway facility using Crystal Lake water. That mix matters when pressure drops or water runs discolored after a break in the street.

If the trouble is on the public side, the town's Public Works department is the right first call. If the trouble is inside your walls, that is where we come in. Permits for repair work come from the town building department, so ask before a rebuild begins.

A railroad town with older cellars

Wakefield grew around the railroad that arrived in 1844, and the Boston Ice Company began cutting ice from Lake Quannapowitt in 1851. Neighborhoods built up that early are a good reason to ask about the age of supply lines and floor drains before a leak turns into a claim.
