Water damage restoration in Melrose Highlands starts with one question: where is the water going next? In a house it moves down through floors and walls. In a condominium building it often crosses into a neighbor's unit, which adds an association and a second insurer to the call.

The Highlands is in Melrose, MA, ZIP 02176, and the call goes through our Melrose dispatch line like the rest of the city. This page covers first steps, what the published record says, and what we could not find.

Where the Highlands sits

Published local history says the Highlands was part of Stoneham until Melrose annexed it on March 18, 1853, making it one of the city's oldest neighborhoods. Roughly, it runs from Main Street and the Horace Mann neighborhood west to the Stoneham line, and from Lynn Fells Parkway north to Wakefield and its Greenwood section.

Water problems do not respect that line. Our crews also work in Stoneham and Wakefield, and a failed street main is a call to the water utility first.

Condominium and shared-wall leaks

A listing-site guide calls the Melrose Towers Condominium, completed in 1972, a fixture of the Highlands. We hold no inspection data on any building here, so this is general guidance, not a statement about one address.

Water from an upstairs fixture usually soaks the ceiling and wall cavities below before a stain shows. Moisture meters and thermal imaging map those cavities, so we check the lower unit even when its owner reports nothing wrong.

First steps when a condo unit leaks

  2. Tell the associationReport it to the property manager or board the same day, in writing, so the discovery time is on record.

Safety first when water meets wiring

If water has reached an outlet, fixture or panel, stay out of the room. Cut power only when the panel is dry and reachable without stepping in water. Otherwise phone the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us.

Anything that backed up from a toilet or drain is contaminated. Keep children and pets away and leave it to a crew equipped for water damage cleanup.

Ell Pond history and its limits

Ell Pond is a 23-acre pond in central Melrose with a 1,100-acre watershed that includes parts of Stoneham and Wakefield. A June 2010 FEMA Region 1 report on the Ell Pond Project lists the Melrose Towers Condominiums among places near it.

That report records up to six feet of water in buildings, yards and streets north of the pond in the 2006 Mother's Day storm, and limited flooding in March 2010 after the city's drainage project. It does not describe damage at specific addresses, so nothing here says any Highlands property flooded.

Flood maps and what is not verified

Flood-zone status for the Highlands as a neighborhood is not verified in anything we reviewed, and FEMA maps covering Melrose were revised on July 8, 2025. Look up your exact address in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer what applies.

We did not find neighborhood-level data on how often Highlands basements or ground floors take on water.

Insurance questions for owners and associations

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst supply line, is often covered, while gradual leaks often are not. Flood is usually excluded and needs separate coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer, since we cannot say what a claim will pay.

Condominium owners often carry their own unit policy, commonly called HO-6, while the association insures the building. Ask both who covers walls, flooring and belongings. Photographs and moisture readings help either insurer.

Drying times and mold

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health mold guide says wet areas should be dried within 24 to 48 hours. Drywall, insulation and carpet dry slowest. If a musty area spreads, see mold remediation, and ask any company whether it works to ANSI/IICRC S500.

Getting to the Highlands

The Melrose Highlands station on the MBTA Haverhill Line and MBTA bus route 131 serve the area. When you call, give the street, building name and floor, plus any entry code or concierge contact.
