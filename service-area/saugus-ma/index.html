Marsh, river and tide: the ground Saugus is built on

Rumney Marsh dominates the south end of town along the Pines River, a tributary of the Saugus River. The Saugus River runs through the town, fed by several brooks, and the town covers about 11.8 square miles, roughly 1.0 of them water. Federal money dredged the river in 2001.

For a homeowner the practical point is that low ground and tidal water are part of daily life here. A cellar near the river can take on water from outside the house even on a day when no pipe has failed.

When water is spreading in Saugus

Water that keeps rising or moving through a floor is an emergency, whether it comes from a high tide, a storm or a split supply line. If water is still spreading, take these steps

These are for the minutes before a crew can reach you. Keep every step on dry footing.

Stop what you can

If a pipe or appliance is the source, shut its valve or the main. Outside water cannot be shut off, so move on to the next steps.

Power only from dry ground

Breakers for wet areas can be switched off if the panel is dry and reachable. A panel in a flooded cellar stays untouched.

Go high

Carry documents, medications and electronics upstairs, and lift furniture onto blocks. Photograph the water level first.

Stay out of the water

Tidal and runoff water can carry sewage and debris, so keep children and pets out.

The floodgate study and what it means for you today

High tides spill into low-lying areas of Everett, Lynn, Malden, Revere and Saugus, according to a January 2024 report in the Lynn Item. In the 1980s the Army Corps of Engineers studied flooding in the area and developed three options, including nine 500-foot gated openings near the General Edwards Bridge.

The Water Resources Development Act of 2022 directs a feasibility study, but the report said no funding had been allocated. Do not plan around a project that does not exist yet. Plan around your own address and your own policy.

Flood damage and your homeowners policy

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage. Some policies offer an endorsement for sump pump failure or water backup, so check the declarations pages or ask your agent. Flood insurance through the NFIP usually has a 30-day waiting period.

Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and photograph the damage inside and out if you ever file. Keep damaged items for the adjuster to see.

Saugus at a glance

These facts shape how a crew approaches a Saugus job.

Neighborhoods

Cliftondale, East Saugus, Lynnhurst, Pleasant Hills, Saugus Center and Oaklandvale all fall inside ZIP 01906, and emergency dispatch available around the clock covers each.

Route 1 corridor

U.S. Route 1 is a divided highway with five exits and Square One Mall, and Route 99 ends at Route 1. Route 107, the Salem Turnpike, crosses the river.

Iron Works

The Saugus Iron Works National Historic Site, in operation from 1646 to about 1670, is a reminder that this river has shaped the town for centuries.

Neighbors

Our crews also cover Wakefield and Malden, plus Revere, ZIP 02151.

Water supply and the town government

Saugus purchases its water from the MWRA, about 3 million gallons a day. A main break in the street is a call to the town, and water inside the home is a call to us. Saugus runs under Plan E, which in 1947 made it the first Massachusetts town to adopt that form of government.
