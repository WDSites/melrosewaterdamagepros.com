What the Main Street historic district means for a water loss

The Melrose Town Center Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on April 1, 1982. It covers about 8 acres of Main Street near the Upham and Essex junction and includes City Hall and Memorial Hall. Main Street is known for Victorian architecture and red brick buildings.

A listing says nothing about how your own building was built. Ask the Department of Inspectional Services what a rebuild at your address needs, since plumbing and electrical permits come from that office.

Safety first when water meets wiring or sewage

Stay out of any room where water has reached outlets, a panel or a light fixture. Cut power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. Phone the utility or 911 for electrical or gas risk before you call us.

Water from a toilet or sewer line is contaminated, so keep children, pets and customers out. Its cleanup is described on our water damage cleanup page.

What the public record says about downtown drainage

Melrose's 2017 to 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report lists the City Hall parking lot as a flood-prone spot, and City Hall sits in the historic district on Main Street. The city won a 2019 MVP Action Grant to design a green-infrastructure fix for that lot.

The report also names Grove Street and a Geneva Road tie-in to Upham Street. Those mark the ends of the downtown strip, but the report does not say the spots are on Main Street. We did not find building-level data, so do not assume your address is or is not affected.

Flood maps and insurance questions

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status for downtown is not verified in anything we reviewed. Look up your exact address in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer how it applies, since maps covering Melrose were revised in July 2025.

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners and commercial policies do not cover flood. Sudden, accidental bursts are often covered, slow leaks often are not. Read your policy and keep photographs. We do not predict what a claim pays.

Drying times and keeping a business open

The state Department of Public Health mold guide says wet areas should be dried completely within 24 hours, with 48 hours in its housing code language. Do not wait on an insurer visit to start emergency water removal.

Our work is measured against ANSI/IICRC S500, a published reference you can ask any company about.
