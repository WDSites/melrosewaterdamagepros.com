What an older house means once it gets wet

A house from the early twentieth century was usually finished with materials that hold water differently from a modern build. Plaster, wood trim and old subfloors can look dry while moisture sits behind or beneath them.

How drying usually works in an older home

This follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard in outline. Your job may differ.

  1. Find the sourceWater has to stop before anything dries. A crew traces the leak, shuts off what can be shut off and notes how far the water traveled.
  2. Extract standing waterPumps and wet vacuums remove pooled water, which cuts the drying load. See emergency water removal.
  3. Check behind surfacesMoisture meters and thermal imaging help show where water went under trim, behind plaster and into wall cavities.

  5. Decide what is removedSoaked insulation and wallboard sometimes cannot be saved.

Painted surfaces and lead rules

The state Department of Public Health mold guide says work disturbing more than 6 square feet of painted interior surface must follow the lead renovation rules at 454 CMR 22.11, which matter most in older homes.

Before demolition, ask how painted surfaces are handled under that rule.

Electrical and sewage safety first

If water has reached an outlet, a panel, a light fixture or an appliance, stay out of the room. Switch off breakers only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.

For any smell of gas or any electrical risk, call the utility or 911 before you call us. Sewage or backup water should be treated as contaminated, so keep children and pets away.

Flood or plumbing: why the cause matters

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, while a sudden and accidental burst pipe is usually covered. Gradual leaks often are not. Some policies add an endorsement for sump pump failure or water backup, so check your declarations pages.

Photographs of the water level and damaged items, taken before cleanup, help the record. Ask your insurer what applies, since we cannot say what any claim will pay. Outside water is covered on our flood damage restoration page.

Drying clock and mold

The Massachusetts DPH guide says wet areas should be dried within 24 hours, and the Housing Code requires areas exposed to leaks and floods to be dried within 48 hours. After flooding that may be hard, so start extraction early.
