Homes, rentals and how water travels between them

In a building with several households, the first question after a leak is who owns which part of the problem. Tenants should tell the landlord or building manager at once and ask them to reach the owner's insurer. Owners should photograph every wet surface before anything is moved.

Water follows pipes, framing and wiring chases downward, so the unit below a leak is often wetter than the unit with the broken fixture. We check both. Moisture meters and thermal imaging find the wet cavities that the eye cannot see, which is why a quiet ceiling stain deserves a call even when the floor above looks dry.

Renters can ask their insurer what a renters policy covers. Owners should read their own policy, since sudden and accidental water losses are often covered and gradual leaks often are not.

When water is spreading through a Malden building

Spreading water is an emergency because it moves through floors and walls into places no one is looking. Call the 24/7 line while the water is still running, not after it stops. The sooner extraction and drying begin, the less drywall and insulation has to come out.

If there is any chance that water has touched an outlet, a panel or a light fixture, stay away and phone the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and we explain the scope and the price before work starts.

If water is still spreading in your unit

Work through these in order, and skip anything that would put you near live power or contaminated water.

Close the source

Turn the fixture shutoff or the unit shutoff to closed. If you cannot find it, ask the manager for the main valve and keep calling.

Mind the power

Use the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can stand clear of water. Otherwise do not touch it and phone the utility.

Raise valuables

Put papers, electronics and bedding on a dry shelf, a bed or a neighbor's floor. Take photos before and after.

Protect people

Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a toilet, a floor drain or the street.

Phone the 24/7 line

Give us the address, the floor and whether water is coming through a ceiling. We take it from there.

The Malden River, the Mystic and the flood outlook

Malden is hilly and sits north of the Mystic River, and the Malden River drains the city. The Mystic River Watershed Association predicts that downtown Malden and Medford will flood on average every five years in the future, according to a March 2022 release from Representative Katherine Clark.

Those are predictions, not a forecast for your street. They are a reason to check the FEMA flood map for your address and to ask your insurer about a separate flood policy, since standard policies do not cover flood damage.

Water service and who to call first

Malden's water is a cooperative effort between the MWRA and the Malden Waterworks Division. If a street main breaks or pressure drops, the Waterworks Division is the first call. Once water is inside your home, our crews handle water mitigation and drying.

Getting to you from the Square to the Orange Line

Routes 28, 60 and 99 cross the city, and U.S. 1 runs through it as a limited-access road. Malden Square, at Main and Pleasant streets, and the 1885 Malden Public Library anchor the center, while the Orange Line stops at Malden Center and Oak Grove. Neighboring Medford and Saugus are covered by the same crews.
