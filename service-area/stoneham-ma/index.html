Winter freezes, spring rain, summer storms and autumn leaves each bring a different kind of wet. Knowing the season helps you spot the likely source, and it tells the crew what to bring.

Winter: frozen pipes and the thaw

A pipe that freezes and splits does its damage when it thaws, often while no one is home. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance reports that 48.6 percent of the state's homeowners' claims in 2024 were for non-flood water losses and freezing-related damage. Coverage for a burst pipe generally depends on the home being properly heated and not left unoccupied.

Open the cabinets under sinks during a cold snap, insulate exposed lines and know where your main shutoff is. If a pipe has already split, close the main valve, then call the 24/7 line and read your policy.

Spring: snowmelt, heavy rain and a damp cellar

Spring water arrives from outside, and that changes how it is treated. Runoff that enters a cellar through a wall or floor is handled as contaminated water, so porous materials such as wallboard, insulation and carpet that cannot be cleaned are removed. The state's homeowner guide on moisture says wet areas should be completely dried in 24 hours and notes that flood-related damage can be difficult or impossible to dry within the 48 hours the Housing Code expects.

Water spreading through a Stoneham house, any season

If water is still spreading in your home

Do these steps in order while you wait. Skip any that mean standing in water near electricity.

Cut the supply

Close the main water valve, and if a frozen line is the cause, leave the faucets open until the crew advises you.

Look at the panel first

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only from a dry floor. If the panel is in a wet cellar, leave it and phone the utility.

Move it up

Stack boxes and furniture on blocks or upstairs, and photograph the water height before you do.

Hold people back

Keep kids and pets out of cellar water, since it may have come from the ground or a drain.

Summer storms and fall gutters

Heavy summer rain can come through a roof edge, a window well or a door sill and soak flooring in an hour. Autumn adds leaves that clog gutters and send water down siding instead of away from the house. Damp framing that stays wet for days invites mold, which needs moisture to grow, so a moisture check after any leak is cheap protection.
