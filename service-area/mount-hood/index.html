The park that gives the neighborhood its name

Mount Hood Memorial Park and Golf Course lies in southeastern Melrose, next to Saugus, according to the Mount Hood Park Association's history. The park has an 18-hole course, picnic areas, an observation tower and several ponds, with Long Pond the largest.

John C. Slayton bought 25 acres there in 1907, and the WPA finished the golf course in 1937. Neighbors across the line can see our Saugus page, and the East Side page covers the Melrose neighborhood closest to Saugus.

What the city records say about ponds and drainage

Swains Pond and Towners Pond are on the east side of Melrose near Mount Hood Golf Club. Melrose's 2017 to 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report lists two drainage concerns tied to them: the outlet structure at Swains Pond and the connection between Towners Pond and Swains Pond.

The report is a city planning document. It does not say which streets or houses those structures affect, and we did not find neighborhood-level data on water entering homes here. So treat it as a reason to ask the city engineering or public works office about your own street, not as a map of damage.

Safety first when water is inside the house

Stay out of any room where water has reached an outlet, the panel or an appliance. Cut power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. If you smell gas or see sparks, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Water that came from outdoors, such as runoff or a pond overflow, can carry soil and sewage, so keep children and pets out of it. Say so when you call.

How storm water usually gets into a house

Heavy rain tends to find the same weak points in any house: window frames, door thresholds, roof flashing, a clogged gutter that spills along a wall and a foundation crack. Our storm water damage cleanup page walks through these cases in detail.

A listing-site guide describes mostly single-family houses along winding roads around the golf course. That description is not verified, so we make no claim about the age, foundations or basements of any particular home. Water rising from the ground is covered on our flood damage restoration page.

Drying, mold and the 48-hour window

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health mold guide says wet areas should be dried completely within 24 hours, and the Housing Code requires areas exposed to leaks and floods to be dried within 48 hours. Flood water can make that window hard or impossible to meet.

Mold needs moisture, so speed matters, and drying is logged against the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. If you already see growth, our mold remediation page explains when a professional is recommended.

Insurance and the FEMA flood map

According to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, flood damage falls outside standard homeowners policies. Some policies add an endorsement for sump pump failure or water backup, so check your declarations pages or ask your agent. Sudden and accidental leaks are often covered, while gradual leaks often are not.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status for Mount Hood is not verified here, and the maps covering Melrose were revised in July 2025. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your exact address and ask your insurer what applies.
