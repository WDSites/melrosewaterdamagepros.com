Emergency Storm Water Damage Cleanup in Melrose, MA

Nor'easters, heavy snow and sudden downpours find the weak spots in a house. The 24/7 emergency line is open during and after the storm.

Storm water damage cleanup deals with water that a storm pushed into the house from the side, from above or through a failed system. Think rain driven through a window frame, an ice dam forcing meltwater under shingles, a limb opening a roof or a sump pump that stopped when the power did. These losses differ from rising water, which is covered on our flood damage restoration page.

During a storm, call as soon as it is safe to do so. We offer same-day response when a crew is available, and heavy weather can mean a queue, so the dispatcher will tell you honestly what to expect. The scope and price are explained before work starts.

Water is coming in around the windows and the walls look dark

Wind-driven rain behaves differently from ordinary rain. It travels sideways and forces water through gaps that never leak in calm weather: window sashes, door thresholds, siding seams and chimney flashing. You often see it first as a dark band under a window or a wet baseboard on the windward side.

That water can run down inside the wall cavity and collect at the bottom plate, so the wall may feel dry on the surface while the framing is soaked. A moisture meter finds the extent, and that reading decides whether the wall can dry in place.

Safety checklist during and after a storm

Weather and water together raise the risk. These steps come first.

  • Do not go outside to inspect damage while wind or lightning continues, and stay clear of downed lines.
  • If water has reached outlets, the panel or a gas appliance, stay out and call the utility or 911 about the risk before you call us.
  • Do not walk through standing water in a basement if the power is still on.
  • Move valuables to a dry floor and photograph all the water damage before cleanup.
  • If a limb has opened the roof, keep everyone out of the room below it.

Ice dams, gutters and meltwater

Winter storms bring their own water problem. Snow melts on the warm part of a roof, runs to the cold eave and refreezes into a ridge of ice. Water backs up behind that ridge, gets under the shingles and drips into the attic and the walls below.

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health homeowner guide lists clogged gutters among the chronic moisture sources in a house. A roofer can clear dams and gutters, and we dry the insulation and drywall that took the water. If the leak is mostly through the roof deck, see our roof leak cleanup page.

How our crew handles a storm loss

  1. Triage by hazardThe crew checks electrical and structural risks first, then works from the wettest and most contaminated areas outward.
  2. Extract and removeStanding water is pumped out and soaked insulation, carpet and board are removed where they cannot dry in place.
  3. Dry the structureAir movers and dehumidifiers run on wet walls, floors and ceilings, with moisture readings logged each visit in line with the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.
  4. Clean and sanitizeSurfaces touched by outdoor water are cleaned and treated, because storm runoff can carry soil and debris.
  5. Plan the repairOnce the building is dry, drywall, flooring and paint are scoped separately so you can compare them with your adjuster's estimate.

When the power fails and the sump stops

A storm that knocks out electricity also stops sump pumps, and a basement can fill within hours. Melrose's 2017 to 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report notes that City Hall lost power during the March 2018 nor'easter and flags six city pump stations as at risk of power loss. If the city's own equipment is exposed, a home pump without a battery backup is too.

Water that comes in this way is outdoor water, so the crew treats it as contaminated until a reading and a look prove otherwise. For deeper basement flooding, our emergency water removal page explains the pumping step.

Storm damage and your policy

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, even away from oceans and rivers. Wind-driven rain through a storm-opened roof is a different question from water rising in a basement. Which one applies depends on how the water entered, so check your policy or ask your insurer.

Some policies add an endorsement for sump pump failure or water backup, so look at your declarations pages. Photograph the damage, including the water height on walls, and keep damaged items for the adjuster.

Mistakes to avoid and how to prepare before the next storm

Do not wait for the weather to clear before calling, because water keeps soaking materials while you wait. Do not run a household vacuum on standing water. Avoid stepping on ceilings or walls that look warped from water.

Before the next nor'easter, clear gutters and downspouts, check caulk and flashing around windows, test your sump pump and consider a battery backup. Keep a flashlight and your insurance details in an easy place.

Melrose storm history that is worth knowing

The city's vulnerability report names the Mother's Day storm of 2006, the heavy snow of winter 2015 and the nor'easters of January and March 2018 as events that stressed the city. The 2006 storm flooded land north of Ell Pond to depths of up to six feet, which led to the city's outlet gate and pipe project.

That project paid off in March 2010, when the pond rose but the West Knoll Soccer Field flooded only at its edges. Those results helped parks and streets. They do not change what a hard rain can do to a window or a basement in your own house, including in neighborhoods such as Mount Hood where the pond outlets are part of the city's drainage concerns.
