Cleanup and sanitizing practice follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which is worth asking any company about.

When wet materials cannot wait

Wet drywall, carpet pad and insulation turn into a problem on a clock. The Massachusetts Department of Public Health guide for homeowners says wet areas should be dried completely in 24 hours, and the state Housing Code expects leak and flood areas dried within 48 hours. After that, mold has what it needs.

That is why emergency cleanup and drying start together. If you find water at night or on a weekend, call the 24/7 line, shut off the source and move what you can off the floor. Anything that has soaked in sewage or flood water should be left for the crew.

How cleanup proceeds

  1. Document before anything movesPhotographs of each room and the damaged items, taken before cleanup, support your claim and your own records.
  2. Remove what cannot be savedWet insulation, pad, soaked wallboard and debris are bagged and carried out, with cuts kept as small as the moisture readings allow.
  3. Clean the surfacesHard surfaces, framing and floors are cleaned so soil and residue do not stay behind to feed odor or growth.
  4. Sanitize where neededSurfaces touched by gray or contaminated water are treated with an antimicrobial product after cleaning.
  5. Deodorize and verifyOdor sources are removed, then moisture readings confirm the area is dry before anything is closed up.

Sanitizing and odor control

Odor after a water loss rarely comes from the water alone. It comes from damp organic material, such as pad, paper and dust, that stays wet inside a cavity. Removing the source works better than covering the smell, and spraying air fresheners over a damp room only hides the warning sign.

Sanitizing matters most with gray and contaminated water. The crew cleans first, because a disinfectant cannot reach through soil, and then treats the surface. Nothing about this step should leave you guessing, so ask your crew which product was used and where.

Documentation for your insurer

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Ask your insurer which applies to you. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance advises photographing the inside and outside, separating damaged items from undamaged ones so the adjuster can review them, and protecting the property from further damage where reasonably possible.

Keep damaged items until your adjuster says they can go, unless they are contaminated or unsafe. A short list with a photo of each major item makes the conversation easier.

Cleanup notes for Melrose homes

When cleanup removes drywall or flooring, the rebuild that follows may need a permit. The Melrose Department of Inspectional Services, also called the Building Department, requires permits to repair, renovate or install new material under the state building code, and separate permits for plumbing, electrical, gas and HVAC work. Ordinary repairs are exempt, as are carpeting, painting and wallpapering. Ask the department before you start rebuilding.

Wet plaster behaves differently from wallboard, and a crew should check it with a meter before deciding to open it. Neighbors in Wakefield and the other nearby towns get the same assessment.
