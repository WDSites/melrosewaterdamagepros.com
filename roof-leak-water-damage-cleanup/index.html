Roof leak water damage cleanup covers the inside of the house after rain, snowmelt or a failed flashing lets water past the roofline. That means wet attic insulation, soaked rafters, a stained or sagging ceiling and sometimes water running down inside a wall. A roof leak is an emergency when water is still entering, when a ceiling is bulging or when light fixtures are wet.

Our job starts below the roof deck. We do not repair shingles or flashing, because that belongs to a roofer. We pull out what is soaked, dry what can be saved and leave the structure ready for repair. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.

A brown stain is spreading and the ceiling feels soft. What is happening?

Water from a roof leak rarely drops straight down. It runs along rafters and the top of the ceiling drywall until it finds a seam, a light fixture or a nail, and then it shows. The stain you see is often several feet from the real opening in the roof.

A soft or bulging ceiling means the board above is holding water. That water has weight, so the section can fall without warning. Attic insulation is usually soaked too, and wet insulation stays wet for weeks because it traps moisture against the framing.

Safety in the first hour of a roof leak

Water above your head and electrical wiring are a bad mix. Work through these points before anything else.

  • Stay out from under a bulging ceiling, and keep children and pets out of that room.
  • If water is dripping through a light fixture or near an outlet, leave the switch alone and call the utility or 911 about the electrical risk before you call us.
  • Cut power to the room only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Place a bucket under active drips and move furniture and rugs to a dry room.
  • Photograph the ceiling, the stain and any wet belongings before anything is moved or removed.

Who does what: your roofer and our crew

Stopping the leak at the roof is roofing work. The Massachusetts Department of Public Health homeowner guide tells residents to hire a professional for roof or siding work, and that kind of job may legally require a licensed contractor. It also lists damaged roofing, failed flashing and clogged gutters as chronic moisture sources, so those are the first places a roofer should look.

Our crew handles the water that already got inside. If a roofer needs a day to schedule, we can still remove wet material and start drying so the damage does not grow while you wait. Heavy wind-driven rain, ice dams and fallen limbs are covered separately on our storm water damage cleanup page.

What happens inside the house

  1. Find the wet areaMoisture meters and a thermal camera show how far water traveled across the ceiling, down walls and through the attic floor.
  2. Open and removeSoaked ceiling board, sagging sections and wet insulation are cut out and bagged so they stop holding water against the framing.
  3. Set drying equipmentAir movers and dehumidifiers run in the room and, where access allows, in the attic space above it.
  4. Monitor the readingsMoisture is logged each visit until framing and board match dry areas of the house, following the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard.
  5. Hand off to repairNew insulation, drywall and paint follow once the roof is fixed. See water damage repair for that stage.

Wet attic insulation and framing

Fiberglass batts that stay wet lose their value, and blown cellulose can clump and hold water for a long time. Both are usually removed when they are soaked through. Rafters and roof sheathing are wood, so they can often be dried in place if the leak is caught early.

Attics are also where small leaks hide. A slow drip over a winter can darken sheathing and leave a musty smell before any stain shows on the ceiling below. If mold has formed, the work follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, and our mold remediation page explains the process.

Roof leaks and your insurance

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, such as a storm that tears off shingles. Gradual leaks from worn roofing, neglected flashing or clogged gutters often are not. Wear and maintenance are the usual dividing line, so your insurer will look at the cause.

Photograph the ceiling and attic before cleanup and keep the roofer's written findings. Check your policy or ask your insurer how it treats roof leaks. Flood coverage is a separate product and does not apply to water entering from above.

Mistakes to avoid after a roof leak

Do not climb onto a wet roof to look for the hole. Do not poke a bulging ceiling with a broom handle unless a bucket is directly underneath and you are standing clear, because the release can be sudden and heavy. Do not paint over a stain while the board is still damp, since the mark will come back and the moisture stays trapped.

Avoid waiting for the next dry week to call. Wet insulation and sheathing begin to grow mold within a couple of days, and the state homeowner guide says wet areas should be dried within 24 hours.

Melrose roofs, rules and older houses

Melrose's 2017 to 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report names the heavy snow of winter 2015 and the nor'easters of January and March 2018 as recent stress events. Heavy snow loads and freeze-thaw cycles are hard on flashing and valleys, so a roof that held up for years can start to leak after a winter like that.

Roof replacement and structural repair usually need a permit from the city's Department of Inspectional Services, which handles building permits in Melrose. The city notes that ordinary repairs are exempt, so ask the department about your specific job before work starts.

Many houses near the old rail stops, such as those in Melrose Highlands, are older. When repairs disturb more than 6 square feet of painted interior surface, the state lead renovation rule at 454 CMR 22.11 applies. That rule can affect ceiling demolition below an old roof, so ask any contractor how they plan to handle it.
