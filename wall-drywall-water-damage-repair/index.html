Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Melrose, MA

Wet walls are opened only as far as the readings require, dried, then rebuilt to match the room.

Call (339) 333-9345

Wall and drywall water damage repair covers the vertical surfaces of a house: drywall, plaster, insulation, trim and the paint on top. In Melrose, wet walls usually follow a supply line failure, a leaking window or siding, an overflowing tub or a flood that reached the lower few feet of a room. The repair begins with finding out how high and how deep the water went.

If water is still running down a wall, call the 24/7 emergency line and shut off the source. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.

The paint is bubbling and the wall feels soft. What happened?

Drywall is a gypsum core wrapped in paper, and paper pulls water upward like a wick. A bubble under paint, a soft patch or a brown ring usually means water is behind the surface. The visible mark often understates the wet area, which can reach several inches beyond the stain.

Safety first: wet walls hide wiring

Walls hold outlets, switches and cable, and water can follow a wire into a box you cannot see. Stay away from a wet wall that has outlets or a switch in it. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before you call us.

Do not poke or press a bulging wall with your hand, because trapped water can burst out. Keep children and pets out of the room, and do not use a wet vacuum near wet outlets.

How a wet wall gets repaired

  1. Find the source and the heightA meter and a thermal camera show how far up the water traveled and whether insulation behind the drywall is wet.
  2. Cut out what failedWet or sagging drywall is cut back to a clean line on the stud, and wet insulation is bagged and removed.
  3. Dry the cavityAir movers and dehumidifiers dry the studs, bottom plate and any remaining board. Readings must match dry areas before anything closes.
  4. RebuildNew insulation goes in, then new board is hung and screwed to the studs.
  5. Finish and paintSeams are taped and coated with joint compound, sanded, primed and painted to match the room.

Flood cuts at two feet or four feet

When water rises a few inches on a wall, a common practice is to cut the drywall horizontally above the wet line so the cavity can dry. These flood cuts are often made around two feet above the floor, and four feet is common when the board is wicking higher or when the sheets run vertically. The cut height is chosen from meter readings, not a rule.

Cutting a clean horizontal line is also easier to patch than a ragged hole in the middle of a wall. The upper board stays if it is dry and sound.

Plaster walls in older Melrose houses

Melrose grew as a commuter suburb once rail stops opened by 1845, so plaster over wood lath is common in older houses, often mixed with drywall from later repairs. In general, plaster can hold water longer than modern board, and it can crack or detach from the lath when it gets wet.

A crew checks it with a meter before deciding whether to open it. Some plaster can be dried and patched, while sections that have softened or come off the lath are removed and replaced.

Insulation and what is behind the board

Wet fiberglass insulation sags, loses its value and holds water against the studs. It is usually removed even when the board in front of it looks fine. In exterior walls, wet insulation also keeps the framing damp and slows drying, so the cavity is opened until the studs read dry.

The state guide says mold can grow in hidden spaces such as inside walls, which is why a wet cavity is never closed up early.

Lead paint and older homes

Work that disturbs more than 6 square feet of interior painted surface must follow the Massachusetts lead renovation rule, 454 CMR 22.11. That matters in older Melrose houses, where cutting or sanding painted walls can create dust. Ask any contractor how they will contain and clean the area before demolition begins.

Permits and who does the work

The city's Department of Inspectional Services says permits are required under the state building code to repair, renovate or install new material, with separate permits for plumbing, electrical, gas and HVAC work. Ordinary repairs and painting are exempt. A drywall patch is usually ordinary, but moving a wall or rewiring a box is not.

The state guide says plumbing, electrical and structural work may legally require a licensed contractor, so the leak itself and any damaged wiring should go to the right trade. Ask your town building department, including in Stoneham or Medford, what your job needs.

Matching texture, primer and paint

A patch that is flat next to a textured wall shows every time the light hits it. The crew matches the texture, whether orange peel, knockdown or smooth, and uses a stain-blocking primer over any old marks so they do not bleed through. A full wall is often painted corner to corner so the new coat has no visible edge.

Mistakes to avoid with wet walls

Do not paint over a damp wall or caulk a seam to hide a stain. Both trap moisture, and the state guide says not to paint or caulk over mold. Do not cut random holes to look for water, since that can hit wiring or pipes. And do not close a wall until the studs are dry.

Insurance and wall repair

The published industry standard for water damage restoration is ANSI/IICRC S500, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowners policy, while slow leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy, take photographs before anything is cut, and ask your insurer what they need.
