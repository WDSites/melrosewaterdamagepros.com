Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Melrose, MA

Cabinets, floors and appliances all hold water. We dry the kitchen and scope the rebuild.

Call (339) 333-9345

Kitchen water damage restoration is the work of drying and rebuilding a kitchen after a sink, dishwasher, supply line or drain leak. The kitchen is one of the wettest rooms in a house, with plumbing in the cabinets, appliances on the floor and finished surfaces that hide moisture. A leak that starts under the sink can be inside the cabinet base, the flooring and the wall within a day.

If water is running or spreading across the floor, close the shutoff valve and call our 24/7 line. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.

What you see and what it usually means

A cabinet door that sticks, a toe-kick that has gone soft, or a floor that rocks under your foot near the sink all point to water that has been sitting. Peeling paint or a dark line at the bottom of a wall next to the cabinets points to wicking from a leak behind them.

A musty smell when you open the cabinet under the sink is a warning on its own. It often means moisture has been present for days or longer, and the cabinet floor and the subfloor under it need a moisture reading, not just a wipe.

Safety first: gas, outlets and standing water

A kitchen mixes water with electrical and gas equipment, so the first steps matter more here than in most rooms.

  • Close the shutoff valve under the sink, or the main water valve if the local valve will not turn.
  • Stay away from the range, outlets and countertop appliances if water is near them, and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before you call us.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility. Do not use switches or phones inside.
  • Cut power to the kitchen only if the breaker panel is dry and safe to reach.
  • Lift small appliances, food and pantry items off wet surfaces and photograph the room before moving anything heavy.

Where the water goes in a kitchen

Water finds the lowest path. In a kitchen, that is usually down the back of the cabinet base, along the toe-kick channel and under the flooring. Cabinets are often installed first and the floor finished around them, so water can run beneath cabinets from one end of a run to the other.

Older Melrose houses often have kitchens that were remodeled over the years, with several layers of flooring or a patched subfloor under the cabinets. That mix of materials dries at different speeds, which is why moisture readings guide the work instead of how the floor looks.

How a kitchen restoration proceeds

  1. Stop and assessThe crew confirms the source is off, checks readings across floor, base cabinets and walls, and maps the wet zone.
  2. Clear the work areaSmall appliances come out and the dishwasher or range is pulled forward if needed. Heavy items are moved only when it is safe.
  3. Extract and removeStanding water is vacuumed. Swollen toe-kicks, wet insulation and soaked padding or underlayment are removed.
  4. Dry in place where possibleAir movers, dehumidifiers and drying panels run for days, with daily readings to confirm progress.
  5. Scope the rebuildCabinet parts, flooring and drywall repairs are listed separately so you can see each stage.

Permits and the order of trades

Kitchen work often needs more than one trade, and the order matters. A plumber fixes the source, restoration dries the structure, and cabinet and flooring installers rebuild. In Melrose, the building department, the Department of Inspectional Services, treats plumbing, electrical and gas work as separate permits, while ordinary repairs, carpeting and painting are exempt.

Ask the department about your specific job before you start. If the water came from a dishwasher or refrigerator line, our appliance leak cleanup page covers that source in detail.

How long a kitchen takes to dry

Most kitchens need several days of equipment, and cabinet cavities and subfloors take longer than open floor. The state mold guide says wet areas should be completely dried within 24 hours, which is the target the crew works toward even when full drying takes longer.

The method is the same equipment-based drying described on our structural drying and dehumidification page, where the moisture-reading targets and equipment are explained.

Insurance questions for a kitchen loss

Insurers generally ask whether the water was sudden or gradual, what the source was and when you first noticed it. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, and long-running leaks and neglect often are not. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

Photograph the room before cleanup, save the failed part and keep receipts for emergency steps. The Division of Insurance advises separating damaged items from undamaged ones so the adjuster can review them.

Mistakes to avoid and how to prevent the next one

Do not cover a wet cabinet base with new flooring or caulk over a gap that is still damp. The mold guide says not to paint or caulk over moisture problems, and trapped dampness grows mold. Do not run the dishwasher to test a repair until the plumber says it is fine.

For prevention, check the supply lines and drain traps under the sink twice a year, replace old braided hoses, and run the exhaust fan while cooking. The guide recommends dehumidifiers and exhaust fans for kitchens, laundry rooms and other humid spaces.
