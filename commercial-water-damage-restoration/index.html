Commercial Water Damage Restoration in Melrose, MA

Commercial water damage restoration is the removal of water from a business space, the drying of the structure and contents area, and the scoping of any rebuild. It follows the same physics as a house, but the decisions around it are different: a landlord, a tenant, a building manager and an insurer may all have a say. If water is still coming in, shut it off where you safely can and call our 24/7 emergency line.

Here is what we do: dispatch a crew to assess the loss, extract standing water, run drying equipment, clean affected areas and write a scope for the rebuild. We explain the scope and price before work starts, and same-day response is offered when a crew is available.

Who does what in the first hour

In a commercial space, the first question is often who controls the water. A tenant may be able to shut a fixture valve but not the building's main valve, and a landlord or building manager may hold the keys to the riser and the mechanical room. Call that person early and say plainly that water is active.

Photograph the damage before anything moves, write down the time you found it and who you told, and keep a copy of the lease and the insurance contact within reach. Those details speed up every decision that follows.

Safety steps before anyone enters a wet space

A wet commercial space can hide electrical equipment, stored materials and slick floors.

  • Keep people out of any area where water has reached outlets, panels, floor boxes or powered equipment.
  • Call the utility or 911 about electrical or gas risks before you call us.
  • If a sprinkler line or fire system is involved, call the building manager or fire department first and do not shut a fire system yourself.
  • Shut off the nearest fixture valve or the HVAC unit only if you can do it from a dry spot.
  • Move records, inventory and electronics up off wet floors if it is safe, and leave heavy items for the crew.

How a commercial loss is handled

  1. Assess the spaceThe crew walks the affected area, checks moisture readings in floors, walls and ceilings, and asks about the water source and what it touched.
  2. Extract standing waterPumps and extraction units pull water from floors, with attention to carpet tile, underlayment and low areas.
  3. Remove what cannot dryWet ceiling tile, insulation and soaked base materials are removed in controlled cuts.
  4. Dry with equipmentAir movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the dry standard, with logs kept for your records.
  5. Scope the rebuildCeilings, walls, flooring and finishes are scoped separately so owners and tenants can see each piece.

Working around operating hours and access

Storefronts along Main Street are covered in more detail on our Downtown Melrose page. A closed shop loses time, so scheduling matters. We can plan drying equipment placement so aisles, entrances and customer areas stay as clear as the job allows, and we will say plainly when an area has to stay closed. Noise and cords are part of drying, and we tell you how long equipment is expected to run.

Keys, alarm codes and building access are a real part of the job. Have the right person available to let the crew in, and tell us about any occupied neighboring units so we can limit disruption.

Insurance and lease questions to ask

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance states that standard homeowners and commercial policies do not cover flood damage, even away from oceans and rivers. Some coverage may exist through an endorsement for sump pump failure or water backup, so ask your agent. Whether a policy covers lost income or closure time is a question for your insurer.

Check who is responsible for what under the lease, and who carries the property policy, you or the owner. We document moisture readings, photographs and equipment placement so you have a clear record to share. We do not make promises about coverage or claim outcomes.

Older buildings in Downtown Melrose

Downtown Melrose is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and many of its buildings are older. Older construction can mean plaster ceilings, mixed flooring over wood subfloors and plumbing that has been altered over time. Those materials dry differently from modern drywall, so readings guide the work.

Melrose's Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report also lists flood-prone locations around the city, so ask your landlord or building manager whether your address has had water problems before.

Permits for rebuilding

Rebuilding often needs permits. The Department of Inspectional Services, the city's building department, requires permits under the state building code to repair, renovate or install new material, with separate permits for plumbing, electrical, gas and HVAC work. Ask the department about your specific job, and ask your landlord who pulls the permit.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

Common mistakes include leaving wet carpet tile in place over a weekend, covering a damp wall with new paint and waiting for the insurer before starting any drying. Moisture that stays trapped grows mold, and the state guide says wet areas should be completely dried within 24 hours.

For prevention, know where the shutoff valves are, test them once a year and keep a contact for the building manager. The drying process matches what is described on the structural drying and dehumidification page, and standing water is handled as described in our emergency water removal work.
