Frozen pipe water damage restoration deals with the leak that appears after the ice melts. A pipe in a cold wall, an unheated garage or a crawl space freezes, the ice pushes the metal until it splits, and the water only escapes once things warm up. In Melrose that usually means a damp ceiling or a wet basement the morning after a cold night turns mild.

If you hear water running in a wall or see a stain spreading, shut the main valve and call the 24/7 emergency line. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.

Why the leak shows up after the cold ends

Ice does not usually break a pipe at the frozen spot. It builds pressure between the ice plug and a closed faucet, and the split opens somewhere along that stretch. While the pipe stays frozen, the plug holds the water in. When it thaws, a crack the length of a thumbnail can release a full line of pressure.

That timing explains a common call: the house was dry at breakfast and wet by noon. Water pours into a wall cavity or floor joists before anything shows, so the first sign can be a bulging ceiling or a drip from a light fixture.

Safety first: thawing and what not to do

A frozen line is not an emergency until it leaks, but how you handle it matters.

  • Never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame to thaw a pipe, because that starts fires and can scald you with steam.
  • Open the faucet the pipe feeds and apply gentle warmth with a hair dryer or warm towels, working from the faucet toward the frozen section.
  • If you see water, shut the main valve first, then stay clear of wet outlets and the panel.
  • If power is involved or you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the utility before calling us.
  • If you cannot find the frozen section or the pipe is inside a wall, stop and call.

Frozen pipe, wet ceiling: what does the stain mean?

A brown ring on a ceiling under a bathroom or an exterior wall often means the pipe above split and has been leaking for some time. A fresh wet patch with dripping means the water is moving now. In either case the cavity above is wet, and the visible stain understates what is behind it.

Pipes in exterior walls, attics and garages are the usual suspects because they sit outside the heated envelope. Melrose grew around the Boston and Maine Railroad stops that opened by 1845, so many houses are older, and older houses are worth checking for pipes in outside walls before a hard freeze.

How the restoration works

  1. Stop and traceThe water is shut off and the crew traces the leak with moisture meters and thermal imaging to find which wall, floor or ceiling is wet.
  2. ExtractStanding water is pumped and vacuumed so the floor stops feeding the wall.
  3. Open the cavitySmall sections of drywall, plaster or ceiling are cut to expose the pipe and wet insulation, which is removed.
  4. Dry with equipmentAir movers and dehumidifiers run until framing and subfloor readings match dry areas, and a plumber repairs the split.
  5. RestoreOnce dry, the area is cleaned and the finishes are replaced under a separate scope.

Cold weather in Melrose and what the city has recorded

Melrose's 2017 to 2018 Municipal Vulnerability Preparedness report names heavy snowfall in the winter of 2015 and nor'easters in January and March 2018 among the events that stressed the city. Those are winters when many homes see long stretches of hard cold, power losses and closed-up rooms.

The practical lesson is that a freeze does not need a deep winter. A night when a wall stays below freezing for hours is enough. If the power goes out in a storm and the heat stops, pipes in outside walls are the first to suffer.

Insurance and heating conditions

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says burst pipe damage is usually covered when it is sudden and accidental, though coverage generally depends on the home being properly heated and not left unoccupied. If you were away and the heat failed, tell your insurer plainly and keep dated records.

Slow, repeated leaks and damage from neglect are often not covered. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and photograph the split pipe and the damage before anything is thrown out.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid before the next freeze

Keep the heat on at a steady setting when you travel, open cabinet doors on cold nights so warm air reaches pipes on exterior walls, and let a faucet drip slowly during a hard freeze. Insulate exposed pipes in garages and unheated spaces, and learn where your main shutoff is.

A mistake we see often is leaving a frozen pipe alone because nothing is leaking yet. Another is treating the visible drip as the whole problem. When water has already reached a wall, the burst pipe water damage cleanup page explains the extraction steps, and water mitigation covers the drying that follows.
