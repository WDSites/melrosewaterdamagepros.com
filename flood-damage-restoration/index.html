Flood damage restoration deals with water that came from outside the plumbing system: storm runoff, groundwater pushing through a foundation, or a sewer line that backed up into the house. In Melrose, flood damage restoration usually means basements, finished lower levels and ground-floor rooms near low spots. Call our 24/7 emergency line as soon as you see water entering the building.

Until the crew arrives, stay out of the water, keep children and pets away, and do not run electrical equipment in a wet space. This kind of water is treated as contaminated. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and the scope and price are explained before work starts.

When flood water is an emergency

Flood water is an emergency from the first inch, because it carries soil, bacteria and whatever else it crossed on the way in. Porous materials it touches, such as carpet, pad, insulation and drywall, are usually removed rather than dried in place. The longer they sit, the more they spread contamination and the more likely mold becomes.

If the water is near the panel, outlets or a gas appliance, stay out, cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk before you call us. Photograph the waterline and damaged items first if you can do it safely.

How a flooded home is restored

  1. Safety and assessmentThe crew checks electrical and gas hazards, identifies the water category and decides what is removed and what can be dried.
  2. ExtractionPumps and extraction units remove standing water. Flood water is moved away from the foundation.
  3. Removal and cleaningContaminated carpet, pad and wet wallboard come out. Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated.
  4. Structural dryingAir movers and dehumidifiers dry framing and subfloors. Readings are logged until materials reach the dry standard.
  5. Hand-off to repairWhen the structure is dry, the rebuild is scoped separately under water damage repair.

Basement flooding cleanup

A flooded basement is a typical flood call in a city built on hills and ponds. Finished basements hold carpet, drywall, insulation and stored belongings that all take water up quickly, and the concrete beneath them holds moisture longer than most owners expect.

Cleanup starts with extraction, then moves everything porous out of the room and checks the wall bottoms for wicking. Boxes of paper and fabric are sorted into what can be dried and what must go. Hard goods like plastic bins and metal shelving are cleaned and kept.

Once the room is empty, drying equipment runs until the readings on the slab, framing and walls match unaffected areas. If mold has started, the work follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 mold standard, and our mold remediation page explains that process.

Raw sewage is coming up from a floor drain: what now?

If sewage is rising in a basement drain or toilet, stop using water in the house, keep everyone out of the room and call. This is Category 3 water, and the work that fixes it is called sewage backup cleanup: extraction, removal of everything porous it touched, and sanitizing of the hard surfaces.

In June 2019 a sewer main blockage on Brazil Street in Melrose sent sewage into at least four homes, and the city reported tree roots and grease in a 113-year-old pipe. Whether a backup comes from the city main or your own lateral changes who is responsible, so note the time, take photographs and tell your insurer.

Flood damage and your policy

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance states that standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, even away from oceans and rivers, and that some coverage may exist through an endorsement for sump pump failure or water backup. Flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program usually has a 30-day waiting period, so it has to be in place before the storm. Check your declarations pages or ask your agent.

Restoration is not waterproofing

Flood damage restoration repairs the damage after water has come in. It does not stop water from entering next time, and we say so plainly. If a basement floods repeatedly, a drainage or foundation contractor is the right trade for that problem, and our crews can document the damage and dry the structure in the meantime.
