Air conditioners pull moisture from the air as they cool, and that water has to drain somewhere. When a condensate line clogs, a pan overflows or a coil freezes and thaws, the water ends up in a ceiling, a closet wall or a floor. AC leak water damage repair is the cleanup and rebuild after that, and it is a seasonal problem in Melrose that peaks in summer.

To be plain about it, AC leaks are less common here than basement, plumbing or roof problems. When they happen, they tend to be slow and hidden. We dry and repair what got wet, and an HVAC technician repairs the unit. The scope and price are explained before work starts.

A stain appeared under the air handler. Is it the AC?

A brown ring on a ceiling below an attic air handler, a damp closet wall near a furnace room or a wet spot under a wall unit usually points to condensate. The drip often shows up on the hottest days, when the system runs longest, and fades when the weather cools.

Condensate water is usually clean to start with, but it can pick up dust and slime from the drain pan. If it sat for weeks, the drywall and insulation may smell musty. A moisture meter separates a fresh leak from an old one.

Where AC water damage really shows up in Melrose homes

Cases are few, but these are the real ones.

Attic air handlers

Central air added to older houses often places the handler in the attic, so a clogged drain line drips onto the ceiling below.

Closet and utility units

A handler in a hall closet can overflow its pan into the wall and floor around it.

Window and wall units

Tilted or overfilled units drip onto sills, plaster and wood floors under the window.

Frozen coils

A coil that freezes during a dirty-filter spell sheds a sudden flood of water when it thaws.

First-hour safety steps for an AC leak

Electrical equipment and water do not mix. Start here.

  • Turn the AC off at the thermostat, and switch off the unit at its own disconnect if you can reach it dry.
  • If water is near outlets or the panel, stay out of the area and call the utility or 911 before you call us.
  • Do not touch a wet unit or wiring, and keep children and pets away.
  • Set a bucket or towels where it drips and move belongings out of the way.
  • Photograph the stain, the unit and the drain pan so you have a record.

How we dry and repair after an AC leak

  1. Locate the water pathMeters and a thermal camera show where condensate traveled through the ceiling, wall or floor.
  2. Remove wet materialsSoaked insulation and soft drywall are cut out, and clean-water carpet pad is judged by readings.
  3. Dry the areaAir movers and a dehumidifier dry the cavity, and humidity is brought down before closing anything up.
  4. Hand off the repairDrywall, paint and trim follow once the structure is dry. See water damage repair for that stage.

Why the drying step matters more than the stain

Condensate often soaks a ceiling slowly, over many days, so the board and insulation are saturated even when the surface looks only lightly marked. The Massachusetts Department of Public Health homeowner guide lists uncontrolled humidity and condensation on cold pipes as chronic moisture sources, which fits this pattern.

Structural drying brings the framing back to normal readings, as the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes. Our structural drying and dehumidification page explains the equipment. If the building is not dried, mold can start, and the work then follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard.

Does insurance cover AC condensate damage?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while slow leaks that go unnoticed for a long time often are not. A one-time overflow is more likely to be treated as sudden than a drain line that dripped all summer. Your insurer decides, so check your policy.

Keep the HVAC technician's findings in writing. They can show what failed and when, which helps when the insurer asks about the cause.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid

Have the condensate line and drain pan checked at the start of each cooling season, and change filters on schedule so coils do not freeze. Consider a float switch that shuts the system off if the pan fills. Do not pour bleach into a drain line without asking your HVAC technician what is safe for the equipment.

Avoid running a unit that is dripping, and do not paint over a ceiling stain while the board is still damp. Dry it first, then seal and paint.

Melrose notes: older houses and permits

Many Melrose houses date from the railroad era, when commuters built near the stops that opened by 1845. Central air in those houses is often a retrofit, so handlers end up in attics or closets that were never designed for water. That is why ceiling stains from condensate are a real, if uncommon, local case.

HVAC work carries its own permit, which the city's Department of Inspectional Services lists separately from building permits. Ask the department before any unit is replaced or relocated.
