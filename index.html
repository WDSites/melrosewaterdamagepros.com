Water damage Melrose

What water damage restoration means

Water damage restoration is the full process of stopping water, removing it, drying the structure, cleaning what it touched and rebuilding what could not be saved. For a Melrose homeowner it usually begins with an emergency call and ends with finished walls and floors, and each step has its own page so you can see what is involved. The published industry standard for the work is ANSI/IICRC S500, and you can ask any company whether its crews follow it.

The order matters. Emergency water removal pulls standing water out first. Water mitigation then dries the framing, subfloors and wall cavities before mold has time to start. Water damage cleanup handles soaked and contaminated materials, and water damage repair rebuilds drywall, flooring and paint.

If your home took in outside water, flood damage restoration covers storm runoff and sewage backups. Where mold has already formed, mold remediation contains and removes it.

Emergency first steps

Emergency water damage: what to do before help arrives

Treat water damage as an emergency if it is still spreading, if it has reached insulation or a subfloor, or if it touched anything electrical. Close the main water valve, lift valuables, and keep your family out of any room with wet outlets or a wet panel. If there is any electrical or gas risk, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Then call the 24/7 emergency line. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and after-hours calls get the same first-step guidance as daytime calls. The state's own mold guide says wet areas should be dried completely within 24 hours, which is why night and weekend calls are not left until morning.

Local to Melrose

Where Melrose water damage starts

Melrose buys its water from the MWRA and runs about 90 miles of its own pipe, so the city is a short distance from a shutoff in most streets, but the line inside your home is yours. Older houses in Melrose Highlands, Cedar Park and Wyoming, built after the railroad stops opened by 1845, often have decades-old supply lines, and the main valve is worth finding before you need it.

Storms are the other source. The city's own vulnerability report lists the 2006 Mother's Day storm, heavy snow in winter 2015 and nor'easters in January and March 2018, and it names flood-prone spots such as Geneva Road at Upham Street and Lebanon Street at Sylvan Street. After a storm like that, check the basement and any ceiling under a roof valley.

Ell Pond, the 23-acre pond in the middle of the city, shows what drainage can do. After the 2006 flood put up to six feet of water north of the pond, the city built an outlet gate and pipe to Lower Spot Pond Brook. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, because a drainage project does not change your policy.

Insurance claims

Document the loss for your claim

Records that help your insurer

We photograph wet areas, log moisture readings and note equipment placement so you have a clear record. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance advises photographing the inside and outside, separating damaged items for the adjuster and protecting the property from further damage where reasonably possible.
